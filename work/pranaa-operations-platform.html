A digital patient journey from intake to outcome tracking, built inside a working clinic.

The client

Prānaa is a regenerative medicine and longevity clinic in Shankar Nagar, Raipur, offering advanced diagnostics, stem cell therapy, energy-based therapies and structured clinical wellness programmes.

Its patient journey is unusually long for an Indian clinic. Rather than a single consultation, patients move through smart intake, targeted diagnostics, multiple consultations with a specialist, nutritionist and longevity coach, advanced pathology, multi-modal treatment and ongoing outcome tracking — across roughly three months.

The problem

A three-month, multi-practitioner journey generates a great deal of information, and every handoff was a chance to lose some of it.

Intake questionnaires were collected separately from diagnostics. Diagnostic outputs — Oligoscan, 4D facial scan, body analyser, bone scan — arrived in different formats from different devices. Three practitioners each held part of the clinical picture. Follow-up across a twelve-week programme depended on someone at the desk tracking where each patient was.

The commercial consequence was worse than the clinical inconvenience: a patient drifting out of a programme in week five was often noticed in week eight.

What we built

A single patient record spanning the full journey, with each stage feeding the next.

Digital intake questionnaires that adapt to the symptoms a patient reports, so the clinical team starts a consultation already informed. Structured capture of diagnostic outputs into one comparable record rather than six device exports. A shared consultation view so the specialist, nutritionist and longevity coach are working from the same picture. Programme scheduling with automated follow-up, so week five is flagged before it is missed. Outcome tracking against biomarkers and progress reports, with monthly assessment points.

Why it worked

Because it was built on the clinic floor, in a clinic where our founder is also technology lead — with the ability to watch a receptionist quietly work around a feature and fix it that week.

What we would do differently

The first version of the intake questionnaire was too thorough. It was clinically excellent and patients abandoned it. Shortening it and moving detail into the consultation improved completion substantially — a reminder that in healthcare software, the clinically ideal design and the usable one are frequently not the same.
