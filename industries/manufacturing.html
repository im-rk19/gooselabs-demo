Manufacturing & Industrial

Factories, plants, steel and production operations.

What we keep finding in this sector

  • Production reality known only through end-of-shift reports
  • Bottlenecks visible in hindsight, never in the moment
  • Safety compliance monitored by spot checks
  • Movement, idle time and material flow entirely unmeasured

Chhattisgarh runs on heavy industry

Steel, power, cement and the supply chains around them. These are operations where a few percentage points of throughput is a large number, and where the floor is usually far better understood by the people standing on it than by the systems reporting on it.

The visibility gap

Most plants know what happened yesterday. Very few know what is happening now.

Reports are assembled at shift end from a mix of sensor data, logbooks and people's recollection. By the time a bottleneck appears in a report, the shift that caused it has gone home. Nobody is lying — the reporting loop is simply slower than the process it describes.

Computer vision as measurement

Cameras already installed for security are an underused source of operational data. Applied carefully, vision analytics can measure movement patterns, idle time, queueing at stations, material flow and safety compliance — continuously, rather than during a spot check.

We start with a bounded pilot on one production area. A pilot that measures one thing well is worth more than a platform that promises to measure everything.

Being honest about limits

Vision analytics works well for movement, presence, counting and zone behaviour. It is much weaker for fine quality inspection unless conditions are tightly controlled, and it is not a substitute for proper sensors on a process that needs precision.

We will tell you which of your questions this technology can actually answer before you spend anything.

On monitoring people

Vision systems in workplaces raise legitimate questions. We design for aggregate operational insight — flow, density, dwell time — rather than individual tracking, and we think that boundary should be explicit and agreed with you in writing before a pilot starts.
