SMEs & Family Businesses

Established Indian businesses running on spreadsheets and institutional memory.

What we keep finding in this sector

  • Critical knowledge held by one or two long-serving people
  • Reporting assembled by hand every week
  • Multiple spreadsheets that disagree with each other
  • Previous software purchases that were never adopted

These businesses work. That is the point.

An established SME turning over ten or fifty crore is not broken. It has survived decades, which is more than most technology companies manage. It runs on relationships, judgement and people who know exactly how things are done.

The risk is concentration. When a business runs on institutional memory, it is one retirement away from a serious problem — and it cannot easily grow beyond the attention of the people holding it together.

Why the last system did not stick

Most SMEs we meet have bought software before and abandoned it. Usually the software was fine. It was designed for a different kind of company, required a full migration before delivering any value, and asked people to change everything at once.

Adoption failures are design failures, not discipline failures.

How we approach it differently

Start with one process, chosen because it hurts. Build around how the business actually works rather than how it should theoretically work. Deliver value in weeks, so people experience benefit before being asked for patience.

Expand only after the first thing is genuinely being used.

Where we usually begin

Automated reporting, because someone is currently spending a day a week assembling it. Or consolidating spreadsheets, because the versions have quietly diverged and nobody trusts the numbers.

Neither is glamorous. Both change how the business runs within a month.

When AI is not the answer

Sometimes the problem is a process nobody has redesigned in fifteen years, and adding AI would automate the waste. We would rather say that in the sprint than build it for you.
