AI Use Case Sprint

Two weeks to find out where AI actually helps your business, and where it does not.

What changes

  • A ranked list of AI opportunities scored on value and feasibility
  • An honest view of which ideas are not worth building yet
  • A costed roadmap you can act on with us or without us

What we deliver

  • Workflow mapping across the functions you nominate
  • Data readiness assessment
  • Opportunity scoring matrix
  • Prioritised roadmap with effort and cost estimates
  • Executive readout session

The most expensive AI mistake is building the wrong thing well

Indian mid-market businesses are under real pressure to "do something with AI". The result is a lot of pilots that work in a demo and die on contact with the operation.

The sprint exists to stop that. Two weeks, fixed scope, fixed price, and a genuine willingness to conclude that the answer is not yet.

What actually happens

Week one — mapping. We sit with the people doing the work and map how information really moves. Where it is entered twice. Where it waits. Where a decision is made from a number nobody trusts. This is floor work, not a workshop.

Week two — scoring and roadmap. Every candidate use case gets scored on business value and technical feasibility, weighted by the state of your data. Ideas that need data you do not have are marked as such rather than quietly assumed.

What you get

A ranked opportunity list, a data readiness assessment, and a roadmap with effort and cost estimates attached to each item.

You can hand that roadmap to us, to your internal team, or to another vendor. It is yours, and it is written to be readable by someone who is not technical.

Why we start here

Because we would rather have a client who builds one thing that works than three that do not. The sprint is also how we decide whether we are the right people for the job.

Common questions

What if the sprint concludes we should not build anything?

Then it has done its job and saved you considerably more than it cost. That outcome happens, and we say so plainly.

Who needs to be involved?

One executive sponsor, and access to the people who actually do the work. The second group matters more - the gap between how a process is documented and how it runs is usually where the opportunity sits.

Is this useful if we already know what we want to build?

Sometimes not, and we will say so. If you have a clear brief and internal agreement, going straight to an MVP build is usually the better use of your money.

Thinking about AI Use Case Sprint?

Tell us what the problem looks like day to day. If we do not think this is the right starting point for you, we will say so.
